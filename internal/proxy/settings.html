Countries

Which country's identifiers the agent recognises, and the thing to settle first: it decides which switches the rest of this tab has to offer at all. Unticking a country removes what only it can find from Personal details, Company identifiers and Banking below; the technical identifiers have no nationality and do not move, and neither do the credentials. What you switch off is remembered across the change, so unloading a country and loading it again does not revive a category you had switched off.

Each country is listed with what it can find. Several of them carry the same category — a postcode, a telephone number, a postal address — and such a category stays while any one of them is loaded.

What gets masked

One switch per kind of value. Unticking one sends those values to the provider in clear, from the next request on.

Secret strength

This grades exactly one of the entries listed below — Secret in an assignment, the catch-all for a value with no known prefix — and nothing else. Every other credential here is recognised by its own shape — a vendor's prefix (dp.pt., A3-, AKCp) or a structure like a PEM block — and is masked at every level whatever this says.

What is always masked

API keys, tokens and connection strings. These have no switch, here or anywhere else: a credential in clear is a live key handed to a provider, and the agent refuses to stop masking one whatever it is asked. They are listed all the same — knowing whether your own vendor's key is among them is a question nothing else here answers, and your browser's find works on the list.

What a masked value is replaced with on its way out.

Changing this clears what has already been substituted, so a conversation in progress starts naming its values differently.
